This is how a case opens.

You are the detective. You read what is in front of you, then decide where to go. You never have time to see everything, so what you choose decides what you know when you name the killer.

Alpine Express, 1.20 a.m.

The Night Sleeper

Sharp knuckles rattle your sleeper door. Outside, frost ferns across the corridor glass as the Vienna-to-Zurich express climbs through a dark gorge.

Monsieur Moreau, the chief conductor, stands in the dim brass glow, his moustache trembling.

MOREAUThank heaven you are aboard, Inspector. Julius Krenn—the art dealer in Compartment 4—has just been found dead. Poison, Dr. Brandt says.

Krenn’s watch lies smashed at 12:38. The train crosses the Swiss frontier at Buchs at two o’clock sharp, when local gendarmes will take over and the passengers scatter.

Only four people share Car B with you and the dead man. You have time for three leads before the frontier brakes grip. Where do you begin?

Pick one. There are five leads and time for three.

What to expect.

A case takes about ten minutes.
It is built for a coffee break.
Every case has more than one ending.
Accuse the wrong person, or the right one without the evidence, and the case ends differently. You can play it again to find the others.
You pick how hard it is.
Rookie explains every clue. Detective leaves you to connect the facts. Mastermind gives no hints and fewer tries at the puzzles.
You don’t need an account.
The whole game plays without one. Signing in only keeps your record if you change phones.
